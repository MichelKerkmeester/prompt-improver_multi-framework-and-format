Design the returns-inspection screen for a 24-inch touchscreen bench station inside a fashion e-commerce warehouse — the single moment a gloved inspector has roughly twenty seconds to compare a returned garment against its original listing and commit to a grade. This is not a data-entry spreadsheet and not a dark developer console; steer hard away from that dense-grid, monospace-chrome utility default. Think instead of a photo-lightbox built for a factory floor — the calm, confident cadence of an airport self-service kiosk crossed with a film-processing light table, built to survive three hundred repetitions a shift without ever feeling like work.

The screen holds two images in quiet dialogue: the customer's original order photo on one side, breathing room around it, and the inspector's own view of the physical item beside it, with the return reason resting between them like a caption, never buried in a table row. Below, four grading actions — A, B, C, Reject — sit as oversized, glove-forgiving tap targets, spaced for a thumb in cotton fabric rather than a mouse pointer, each confirming with an immediate, satisfying weight-shift instead of a spreadsheet checkmark. Choosing Reject opens one unhurried camera moment for a single damage photo, then hands the rhythm straight back.

Every tap should feel instant and certain, the interface acknowledging the inspector rather than making her wait, and any motion respects reduced-motion preferences rather than performing for its own sake. Colors stay warm and matte, not clinical, not neon-on-black, holding strong contrast between photo, caption, and buttons under harsh warehouse lighting. Keep the layout resilient from the fixed bench screen down to a narrower supervisor tablet view, and give every tap target a visible focus state for staff who navigate by scanner or keyboard. The kind of station that makes hour eight feel exactly like hour one. Use shadcn/ui components throughout, customized rather than left as an untouched default surface.
