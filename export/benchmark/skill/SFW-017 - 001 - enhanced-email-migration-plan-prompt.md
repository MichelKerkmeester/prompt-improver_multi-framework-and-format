Mode: $improve | Complexity: High (8/10) | Framework: CRAFT

**Context:** A Dutch housing association with offices in Zwolle and Deventer is migrating email from an on-premises Microsoft Exchange Server 2016 environment (past Microsoft's extended support since October 2025) to Microsoft 365. Migration scope is 1,150 user mailboxes and 60 shared mailboxes (1,210 mailboxes total). One shared mailbox handles customer service and is business-critical. Of the 1,150 users, 80 are field staff who work exclusively from mobile phones, with no desktop or laptop access, so their mailbox access, testing and support must work in a mobile-only context. Hard constraints: cutover of end users from on-premises to Microsoft 365 may only happen during weekends; the customer-service mailbox may be unavailable for no more than 2 hours total during its own cutover; the entire migration must finish within 6 weekends. Mailboxes contain tenant personal data, so data handling during migration should respect GDPR/AVG obligations. The specific migration technique (staged, cutover batches, hybrid, or another sound method) is not yet decided — choose and justify the method that best satisfies the constraints below rather than assuming one.

**Role:** Act as a senior Microsoft 365 messaging migration architect with hands-on experience running phased Exchange-to-Exchange-Online cutovers for mid-size Dutch organizations, skilled in mailbox batching strategy, minimal-downtime cutover design, mobile-only user enablement, and stakeholder change communication.

**Action:** Produce a complete, phased migration plan that moves all 1,210 mailboxes from Exchange 2016 to Microsoft 365 within 6 weekends, satisfying every constraint in Context. Specifically:
- Sequence the migration into logical phases (for example: readiness/preparation, pilot batch, production batches, decommission) and map each production phase to one of the six available weekends.
- For every phase, define: its objective and which mailboxes or users are in scope; entry criteria that must pass before the phase may start; exit criteria that must pass before the phase is declared complete; a rollback step that reverts that phase's changes without data loss if exit criteria are not met; and a staff communication moment that happens before the phase begins, naming the audience, channel, timing relative to the phase, and the core message.
- Give the customer-service mailbox its own explicit sub-plan showing how downtime will be tracked during its cutover, including a go/no-go checkpoint partway through the 2-hour window.
- Give the 80 mobile-only field staff their own explicit sub-plan covering mobile client configuration and testing before cutover and mobile-specific support during the first week after their batch.
- Build one consolidated risk table for the whole migration (not per phase) with columns for risk, likelihood, impact, mitigation, and owner, covering at minimum: weekend-only timing pressure, the customer-service downtime ceiling, mobile-only field staff, mail loss or duplication, and Exchange 2016's end-of-support status.
- Close with a section that maps each success target below to the specific phases or checks designed to protect it.

**Format:** Structure the plan with these sections, in order: Executive summary (roughly 150 words); Weekend-by-weekend batching schedule as a table with columns Weekend #, Relative timing, Phase, Mailbox batch/scope; Phase detail, one block per phase using consistent sub-headers (Objective, Entry Criteria, Exit Criteria, Rollback Step, Pre-Phase Communication); Customer-service mailbox sub-plan; Mobile-only field staff sub-plan; Risk table (single table as specified above); Success-target traceability section.

**Target:** Primary readers are the housing association's internal IT team, who will execute the migration and are accountable for it, plus business stakeholders who need visibility into cutover weekends and customer-service risk. The plan succeeds if a reader can tell, without needing clarification, exactly when each phase runs, what must be true to start and finish it, how to roll it back, who is told what and when, and how the plan protects: zero lost mail across the cutover, fewer than 5% of the 1,210 users logging a migration-related support ticket in the first week after their cutover, and full completion within the six-weekend window.
