Mode: $improve | Complexity: High | Framework: CRAFT

**Context:** A Dutch housing association (woningcorporatie) with office locations in Zwolle and Deventer is migrating email from an on-premises Exchange Server 2016 environment to Microsoft 365. Scope is 1,150 user mailboxes and 60 shared mailboxes (1,210 mailboxes total). All cutover activity (the actual switch of a mailbox or batch from on-premises to Microsoft 365) may only happen on weekends; no cutover work runs on weekdays. The customer-service mailbox is business-critical and may be offline for no more than 2 hours during its own cutover. Of the 1,150 users, 80 are field staff who access email exclusively through phones (no desktop or laptop mail client), so they must retain working mail access across their cutover window. The full migration, across all mailboxes, must be completed within a maximum of six weekend cutover windows. If any input needed to sequence the plan is unstated (for example directory-synchronization status, network link capacity between Zwolle and Deventer, or current Exchange 2016 patch level), state that assumption explicitly in the plan rather than silently guessing.

**Role:** Act as a Microsoft 365 messaging migration architect with hands-on experience running hybrid Exchange-to-Exchange-Online cutover migrations for multi-site organizations, including weekend-constrained cutover windows and mixed desktop/mobile-only user populations.

**Action:** Produce a phased migration plan that:
- Breaks the migration into a sequence of phases (choose the phase count and content that best fits the six-weekend budget and the mailbox volume) covering everything from pre-migration assessment through post-cutover stabilization and decommission of the legacy environment.
- For every phase, specifies: the phase objective, entry criteria (what must be true/ready before the phase can start), exit criteria (what must be verified before moving to the next phase), and a rollback procedure describing exactly how to revert that phase's changes if it fails.
- For every phase, defines one staff communication moment that happens before that phase's cutover work begins: who is notified, what channel is used, and a one-line summary of the message content.
- Explicitly identifies which phase(s) the customer-service mailbox migrates in and describes how that phase's execution keeps its downtime at or under 2 hours.
- Explicitly identifies how the 80 phone-only field staff retain mail access during their cutover phase(s), covering mobile client reconfiguration and continuity of access.
- Confirms that all cutover work across all phases fits within six weekend windows total, and states which phase(s) map to which weekend(s).
- Includes a consolidated risk table covering migration-specific risks (for example mail flow interruption, calendar/free-busy breakage, mobile device re-enrollment failures, DNS/Autodiscover cutover errors, data loss during batch moves) with columns: Risk, Likelihood, Impact, Mitigation, Owner.

**Format:** A single structured Markdown migration plan document containing, in order: a brief overview of the phase sequence and its mapping to the six weekend windows; the full phase-by-phase detail (objective, entry criteria, exit criteria, rollback procedure, pre-phase staff communication) for each phase; the consolidated risk table; and a closing section that restates the three success targets and how the plan verifies each one during and after migration.

**Target:** The plan is ready to execute as written and is designed to achieve zero lost mail (no messages or items missing when comparing pre- and post-migration mailbox contents), fewer than 5% of the 1,150 users submitting a support ticket in the first week after their cutover, and full completion of all 1,210 mailboxes within six weekend cutover windows, while keeping the customer-service mailbox's downtime at or under 2 hours during its cutover.
