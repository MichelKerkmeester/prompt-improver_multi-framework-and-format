Build a morning dashboard for the owner of a small bakery chain. For each of her 3 shops it shows daily sales, stock that is running low and tomorrow's pre-orders.

Used by the owner, on an iPad at 6am before the shops open, to check all three shops at a glance.

It should feel warm and calm, like a bakery in the quiet hour before opening: ovens warming, the street still dark, everything in its place.

Steer away from two defaults: the SaaS analytics tool, with its grid of KPI tiles, trend charts and filter bars, and the "warm startup" look of cream, high-contrast serif and terracotta. The numbers read as plain, confident statements, not charts to decode.

One aesthetic risk: take the palette from the hour itself, the soft blue-grey of the sky before sunrise as the ground and the pale gold of the first oven light as the only accent, on a low-glare surface gentle on just-woken eyes.

The three shops sit side by side as equals, each reading top to bottom in the same order (sales, running low, tomorrow's pre-orders), so comparing shops is a glance across. Generous breathing room, a rounded, humane sans-serif and numerals large enough to read without leaning in.

When she opens it, the shops appear softly one after another, like lights coming on. Running-low stock carries a quiet gold marker, never an alarm red or a blinking badge. Touches respond gently, without hurry.

Constraints: iPad first, adapting to landscape, portrait and other screen widths. Every interactive element shows a visible focus indicator. With reduced motion on, the view simply appears. All text, gold included, meets WCAG AA contrast.

The kind of screen that feels like a note from a trusted manager left on the counter: everything she needs, nothing that shouts.
