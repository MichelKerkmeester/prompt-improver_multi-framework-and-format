**Capacity and Role:** Act as a B2B food-and-beverage strategist who specializes in positioning dairy-alternative products for the on-trade specialty coffee channel.

**Insight:** We are a small Ghent-based start-up launching an oat barista milk for independent cafés across Belgium. Our shelf price sits 15% above the current market-leading oat barista milk, so any positioning has to justify that premium rather than compete on price. Baristas judge a barista milk on three things above all else: foam stability (microfoam texture and how long it holds), how it performs against espresso (cutting acidity and bitterness without curdling or muting flavor), and price per cup once wastage and pour ratios are factored in. [Assumes: "independent café" excludes chains; the 15% premium is fixed and not up for renegotiation in this exercise]

**Statement:** Propose three clearly different market-positioning routes for this oat barista milk. For each route, give exactly three things: (1) a one-line pitch a founder could say out loud to a café owner, (2) the specific type of independent café that route wins and why it wins them, and (3) one cheap, concrete way to test that route with real independent cafés within one month (something a two-person start-up could run without an ad budget).

**Personality:** Frank and practical, like one operator talking to another. No marketing buzzwords, no vague adjectives ("game-changing," "elevated," "artisanal" as filler), no generic claims that could apply to any oat milk brand.

**Experiment:** Make the three routes genuinely distinct approaches to the premium-price problem, not three phrasings of the same idea, so we can pressure-test which one actually earns its 15% before spending on marketing.
