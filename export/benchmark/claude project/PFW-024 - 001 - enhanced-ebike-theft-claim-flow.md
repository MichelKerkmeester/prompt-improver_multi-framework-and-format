Design the five-screen theft claim flow for an e-bike insurance app's mobile experience — built for a commuter standing at an empty station rack, phone in hand, adrenaline still up, with one job only: file a complete theft claim before her tram arrives, in under five minutes.

This reads like an intake desk that has done this a thousand times, not a lifestyle app asking her to get started. Borrow the quiet authority of an airport lost-and-found counter or a precinct evidence window — procedural, unhurried in tone, fast in outcome. No confetti, no progress-bar cheerleading, no mascot relief for a completed field; she is reporting a theft, not levelling up.

Five distinct moments, one screen each:
1. **What happened** — a single uncluttered question in her own words, nothing else competing for attention.
2. **Where and when** — her station already resting in the location field, pulled quietly from where she stands, editable, never demanding re-entry; time defaults to now.
3. **Photos and frame number** — a capture zone that reads like an evidence sleeve, the frame number resting just beneath it.
4. **Police report** — a number field beside an equally weighted "I'll add this later," so a missing report never blocks the claim.
5. **Confirmation** — a live tracker showing the claim already moving.

Every back arrow rewinds without erasing; she can retreat a screen and find her words untouched. If the connection drops between rack and platform, her draft is already saved — she resumes mid-sentence, not from zero. Transitions move like one deliberate step forward: quick enough to respect five minutes, never bouncy, never celebratory.

Typography speaks level and unhurried. Color sits closer to overcast daylight than sunset — graphite and paper, with a single hazard-amber signal held back for progress and confirmation only, the one warmth in the interface, so progress reads as unmistakably real.

Every screen ships in Dutch and English from the same layout grammar, holding its calm structure whichever language runs longer. No prescribed component library — let the interface's own language emerge. Steer hard away from the gamified-wizard default and the generic fintech gradient; this is a claims desk, not a landing page. It should hold together as comfortably on a cracked screen at a bus stop as on a tablet at home, with visible keyboard focus, restrained motion, and text that reads clearly in poor light.
