**Capacity:** Act as a B2B food-and-beverage strategist who has sold ingredients and dairy alternatives into independent café accounts, working here as a sparring partner for a small Ghent-based oat milk startup, not as a hired agency pitching back generic ideas.

**Insight:** The startup makes an oat barista milk for independent cafés across Belgium, priced 15% above the category's market leader. At that premium, an idea only survives if it holds up with the person actually pulling shots, so ground every idea in what a barista notices first: foam stability under the wand, how the milk tastes once it hits espresso (not tasted alone), and price per cup rather than price per liter, since per-cup cost is what makes a 15% list-price gap disappear or explode.

**Statement:** Produce three positioning routes for entering the Belgian independent café market at this premium. The routes must be clearly different from each other, not variations on the same angle. For each route, give exactly:
- A one-line pitch, phrased the way a rep would actually say it to a café owner
- The specific type of café or barista it wins (skill level, menu style, or stated values — not a generic label like "trendy cafés")
- A cheap way to test that route with real cafés within one month, using only resources a small startup already has

**Personality:** Frank and practical. Write like someone who has actually walked into cafés and pitched dairy alternatives, not a brand deck. No buzzwords or filler ("elevate," "unlock," "seamless," "journey"). Short, direct sentences.

**Experiment:** Design each cheap test so it answers one specific question: would this route make a barista accept paying 15% more per cup — not whether oat milk in general is acceptable. A test that only proves the milk performs is not enough; it must show the specific positioning story is what lands.
