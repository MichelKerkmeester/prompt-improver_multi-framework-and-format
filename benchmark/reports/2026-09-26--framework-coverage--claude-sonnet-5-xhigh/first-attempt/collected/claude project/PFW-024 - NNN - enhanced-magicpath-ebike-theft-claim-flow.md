Design a five-screen theft-claim flow for an e-bike insurance app, built for a commuter who just watched an empty rack where her bike used to be—phone in hand, adrenaline still up, needing this filed before her next train. The whole journey must resolve in under five minutes, so every screen exists only to remove one obstacle between her and a filed claim, never to charm her.

This is not Duolingo's staircase of unlocked wins, and it is not another fintech gradient promising "you've got this." Steer away from progressive-reveal celebrations, confetti checkmarks, and centered-hero cheerfulness entirely. Think instead of the quiet authority of a police intake desk crossed with the unfussy precision of a well-run dispatch log—competence that doesn't need to perform itself.

Layout stays low and close: each screen's single task sits within thumb's reach at the frame's base, station map and camera both anchored where her hand already rests, asking nothing of her but a glance and a tap.

She opens on "What happened," a short, factual account with no cheerful prompts—just space to state the theft plainly. It leads to "Where and when," her last known station already filled in from her location, waiting only for confirmation and a time. Next, photos and the frame number, the camera opening as naturally as unlocking her phone. Then a police report field that accepts a number now or defers it—"add later" reads as competent, not apologetic. She arrives at confirmation: a live tracker showing her claim in motion, proof that filing was enough.

Every screen threads back to the last without losing a keystroke, and if her signal drops mid-station platform, the draft waits for her exactly where she left it—reassurance stated plainly, not as an alarm.

Visual language: overcast slate and graphite, with a single muted rust accent reserved only for active-progress states—like a case-number stamp, not a call-to-action, because color here owes her proof, not persuasion. Typography is quiet and upright, closer to an official form than a friendly assistant. Motion is brisk and linear, never bouncy—pages turn like a case file, not a stories reel.

The interface speaks Dutch and English interchangeably, a toggle as unremarkable as switching hands on the handlebar. Legible in daylight glare, thumb-operable one-handed, adaptable to a larger screen, with visible focus states and minimal motion for anyone who needs stillness.

The kind of claims flow that makes a stressful five minutes feel already handled.
