**Context:** A beginner-focused coffee blog needs an introductory article for readers who enjoy coffee but have never brewed it deliberately. They have no specialty equipment and no vocabulary for grind, ratio, or extraction. The post is the reader's first step from "coffee happens to me" to "I can make a good cup on purpose."

**Objective:** Write one complete blog post that teaches absolute beginners how to brew coffee at home. It must explain the core variables that determine taste (coffee-to-water ratio, grind size, water temperature, brew time), walk through two beginner-friendly methods (French press and pour-over) step by step, and give a single recipe the reader can succeed with on the first attempt.

**Style:** Practical and jargon-free. Define every technical term in plain language on first use. Use short paragraphs and descriptive subheadings rather than dense blocks. Include a numbered recipe the reader can follow without re-reading.

**Tone:** Warm, encouraging, and confidence-building, like a knowledgeable friend. Never condescending. Normalize beginner mistakes instead of warning the reader away from them.

**Audience:** Complete beginners, roughly 20-45, who brew coffee occasionally and want a reliably better cup. Assume no prior knowledge and no equipment beyond a kettle, a mug, and either a French press or a basic pour-over cone.

**Response:** A [800-1,200]-word blog post containing:
- An opening hook that names the beginner's real frustration, such as coffee that never tastes as good as a cafe's.
- Clear H2/H3 subheadings for skimmability.
- A short "what you actually need" list with budget-friendly notes.
- One starter recipe as numbered steps with exact ratios and times.
- A brief "common beginner mistakes and quick fixes" section.
- A closing call to action inviting the reader to try exactly one method this week.

Do not include affiliate links, health or medical claims, or advanced espresso-only techniques.
